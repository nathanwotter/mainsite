# De-identified instructor response bank

These are excerpts/adaptations of Nathan Williams's actual Moodle replies from PRT 152 proposal forums. Student names, peer references, and identifying details have been removed; light cuts are used where a reply contained unrelated personal context. They are **style and decision examples**, not standing assignment rules. Current forum instructions prevail.

Source forums reviewed: [Spring 2023](https://moodle-courses2223.wolfware.ncsu.edu/mod/forum/view.php?id=662052), [Fall 2023](https://moodle-courses2324.wolfware.ncsu.edu/mod/forum/view.php?id=398926), [Spring 2024](https://moodle-courses2324.wolfware.ncsu.edu/mod/forum/view.php?id=727606), [Fall 2024](https://moodle-courses2425.wolfware.ncsu.edu/mod/forum/view.php?id=27839), [Spring 2025](https://moodle-courses2425.wolfware.ncsu.edu/mod/forum/view.php?id=697525). Fall 2025 and Spring 2026 forums were also inspected for recent practice; see the pattern notes below.

## Novelty and scope

- **Spring 2023 — previously familiar activity:** “You'll need to clarify a bit more how this will be a new activity added to your leisure toolbox (remember that the assignment is focused on trying something new, not re-establishing activities you've done before).”
- **Fall 2024 — several possibilities:** “You'll just need to pick one of these and describe the structure for adding the activity to your leisure toolbox. Remember that the purpose of the assignment is to try a new leisure activity to you (not improve something or do more of something you already know).”
- **Fall 2024 — familiar sport with a possible new form:** “You'll need to clarify a bit more what the new aspect of fishing will be, since this assignment is about adding a new activity to your leisure toolbox. Please follow up with a revised proposal.”
- **Spring 2023 — wellness alone is insufficient:** “Remember that this is a chance to expand your leisure and recreation toolbox, not just benefit your wellness. Can you clarify a bit more what additional activity you'd be learning to do or trying out?”
- **Spring 2023 — non-leisure alternatives:** “Networking and volunteering are great activities to do but aren’t a new leisure activity. Please revise your proposal with something that will expand your leisure toolbox and let me know any questions you have!”

## Learning structure and repeated experience

- **Spring 2023 — art-making:** “You'll need to identify the structure you're using to learn painting a little more specifically. It could be a class/workshop at the Crafts Center or you can find an online class ... to complete. This will create some specific activities for you to complete. I'll expect that you do the class activities and try producing 3 different paintings ... on 3 different days.”
- **Spring 2025 — music production:** Nathan asked for a structured course or sequence, rather than a loose collection of videos, so the student would learn and practice identifiable techniques.
- **Fall 2024 — figure skating with a friend:** “Please include details of the ‘lessons’ that your friend gives you (i.e., how are you learning the techniques for doing it well?). I'll expect that you try figure skating on 3 separate days across subsequent weeks (i.e., not crammed together all at once).”
- **Fall 2024 — sampling classes:** “Rather than sampling a bunch of different classes, please select one type of class you'd like to attend multiple times. You’ll need to attend at least 3 classes across different weeks to get a sense of what adding these to your leisure repertoire is like.”
- **Fall 2024 — tennis:** “You will need to apply a bit more structure to it (i.e., how are you learning the rules/techniques for doing it well?). This could be an online class/coach or an in-person learn-to-play tennis experience, but it needs to be a bit more structured than just going out with your friend.”
- **Spring 2023 — drawing follow-up:** “Sure, that works provided that you're spreading out the drawing across multiple days/weeks (since the purpose of the assignment is to see what this is like as a leisure activity, not just produce the paintings).”

## Activity-specific precedents, not universal thresholds

- **Spring 2023 — reading:** “Since reading can be hard to create structure around (and we all know how to read in this class), that one typically doesn't work unless you have a book club or other social activity tied to reading the book.”
- **Spring 2023 — guitar:** “Learning to play guitar would meet the criteria ... if you clarify the structure for how you'll be learning it (i.e., sequenced online course, workshop ... individual lessons, etc.).” Nathan requested a short playing video for that student's final submission, not merely a photo with the instrument.
- **Spring 2024 — hiking:** Nathan suggested three distinct hiking destinations on separate days, so the student would experience the activity as a potential ongoing leisure option.
- **Fall 2024 — pickleball:** “You’ll need to try playing pickleball at least 3 times, for at least an hour each time. I'll just ask that you spread these out over multiple days ... In your reflection, please include how you learned the rules of the game.”
- **Fall 2024 — climbing:** “Try the roped climbing area at least 3 times ... and set some kind of goal that will indicate your increased comfort with this activity. It could be number of climbs you complete per day or something like that.”
- **Spring 2025 — knitting:** “I'll expect that you include the techniques you learn in your reflection paper and document trying knitting at least 3 times for an hour each time (with pictures of your progress along the way!), in addition to completing a small project.”
- **Fall 2024 — yoga:** “You’ll need to attend at least 3 classes across different weeks to get a sense of what adding these to your leisure repertoire is like.” Nathan explained that independent reading about yoga could supplement, but not replace, the classes in that plan.

## Tone and follow-up

Nathan often opens with a specific, encouraging observation (“That sounds like a fun addition to your leisure toolbox!”), makes one or two concrete requests, and ends with “Let me know any questions you have or how I can help.” His replies are direct when an activity misses the assignment purpose, but offer a path to revise. Very short acknowledgments (“That sounds great!”) occur after a student has already clarified an earlier question; do not use them for a first response that still needs substantive feedback.

Across Fall 2025 and Spring 2026, recent replies continued to emphasize a genuinely new activity, a learning route (class, club, workshop, instructor, sequenced tutorial, etc.), and enough attempts to test whether it belongs in the student's leisure repertoire. For outdoor activities, Nathan sometimes suggested distinct routes or destinations; for crafts, tangible practice sessions or products. Treat these as examples to adapt, not automatic quotas.
