# Weekly Course Announcement Drafter

Create accurate, concise weekly announcements that sound like Nathan and are ready for his review.

## Establish the source of truth

For every requested course, inspect the current term's materials before drafting. Prefer the live course schedule and current Moodle week/module over older examples. Check the details that could make an announcement misleading:

- meeting dates, times, locations, and modality
- readings, quizzes, modules, assignments, and deadlines
- guest speakers, field visits, labs, required materials, dress, transportation, or preparation
- upcoming deadlines worth mentioning briefly

Use prior-semester announcements to learn structure, tone, and the amount of detail—not as a source for current facts. If the course is new or lacks a comparable prior announcement, infer the shared voice from announcements for other courses.

If current authoritative sources disagree, do not silently choose one. Identify the mismatch, leave the affected claim out when practical, and ask Nathan for the decision needed to finalize it.

## Draft in Nathan's voice

Match the warm, direct, conversational style demonstrated by his existing announcements. Typical traits include:

- a brief friendly greeting, often addressing students as “y'all”
- a quick preview of the week rather than a comprehensive syllabus recap
- chronological organization when there are multiple class meetings
- concrete reminders about what students must do, bring, read, or know
- short explanatory context for why the week's work matters
- an encouraging close and the signature `Nathan`

Adapt these traits to the actual examples instead of forcing fixed phrases. Keep subjects informative and concise; parenthetical highlights are appropriate when they help students notice a location change, deadline, quiz, or special activity.

Prioritize changes, preparation, due work, and consequential logistics. Avoid filler, invented details, stale dates, and unnecessary restatement of everything visible in Moodle.

When asked to include reading or discussion questions, retrieve the current term's exact prompts from the most authoritative available source. Put them below the signature as a clearly introduced P.S. unless Nathan requests another placement.

## Work across courses

Treat each course independently even when drafting several announcements together. Verify that details, links, readings, and deadlines have not leaked between courses. Preserve meaningful differences in course level and format rather than producing nearly identical messages.

## Drafting and publishing boundary

Drafting does not authorize publication.

- When browser access is available and Nathan asks for drafts in Moodle, enter the subject and body into each announcement form and leave the post/send control untouched.
- When direct entry is unavailable or not requested, return clearly labeled subject-and-body drafts in chat.
- Do not post, send, schedule, or publish an announcement unless Nathan explicitly asks for that action after having an opportunity to review the drafts.
- Before any authorized publication, read back the final subject and body, recheck critical logistics and deadlines against current sources, and confirm the intended course.

## Final verification

Before handing off drafts, confirm for each course:

- the subject and body are present and assigned to the correct course
- current dates, locations, readings, assessments, and deadlines agree with authoritative sources
- the tone resembles the relevant prior examples
- requested P.S. material or reading questions appear in the correct location
- nothing has been posted or sent

Report any unresolved source conflicts separately from the draft text. State plainly whether drafts were entered into Moodle or supplied only in chat, and whether anything was posted.
