# Video Question Planner

Turn an instructional video into a practical, timestamped question-placement guide without interrupting the lesson unnecessarily.

## Establish the review target

Identify the video, intended learners, learning purpose, and any constraints the user provides about question count, question types, grading, or platform. If those details are absent, review first and recommend an appropriate interaction density rather than stopping for routine clarification.

Use the video's actual spoken and visible content as the source of truth. Obtain a timestamped transcript when available. If reliable captions are unavailable, transcribe the audio when the environment permits. Review relevant visual context as well when slides, demonstrations, diagrams, or on-screen actions affect what students can answer.

Confirm the video's duration and keep timestamps aligned to the playable version. Do not invent exact wording or timing when access is incomplete; state the limitation and use approximate timestamps only when clearly labeled.

## Find meaningful pause points

Classify candidate moments as one of these:

- **Explicit:** the instructor directly asks students a substantive question, tells them to respond, or assigns a brief task.
- **Suggested:** a question would materially improve retrieval, comprehension, application, reflection, or transition between major ideas.

Do not treat rhetorical questions, conversational remarks, verbal fillers, or navigation comments as learning checks. Favor moments after a complete explanation, definition, worked example, comparison, or major section—not in the middle of a sentence or thought.

For explicit moments, preserve the instructor's intent while tightening wording when necessary for clarity. For suggested moments, make the purpose evident and avoid asking students to know material that has not yet appeared.

## Design the questions

Choose the question format that best fits the learning objective and the preceding segment:

- open response for reflection, career connection, personal application, synthesis, or idea generation
- multiple choice for distinctions, definitions, misconceptions, and quick comprehension checks
- matching or scenarios for roles, categories, processes, and applied judgment
- confirmation prompts only when students must complete an external activity that cannot be captured in the video platform

Keep questions concise and answerable at the proposed pause point. Supply the correct answer and a brief rationale for objective questions. For open responses, give a focused prompt rather than a broad invitation to summarize everything.

Avoid excessive interruption. When several candidates cluster together, keep the strongest one. Aim for meaningful spacing across major sections rather than enforcing a rigid questions-per-minute rule.

## Deliver the placement guide

Provide a concise table with:

- pause timestamp
- explicit or suggested classification
- question type
- ready-to-use question text
- correct answer, rationale, or implementation note when useful

After the complete guide, recommend a lighter subset for users who want fewer interruptions. Explain briefly why those placements provide good coverage. Mention any candidate moments deliberately excluded because they were rhetorical, repetitive, premature, or too close to a stronger question.

Do not upload questions, modify the video, or publish changes unless the user explicitly requests that separate action.
