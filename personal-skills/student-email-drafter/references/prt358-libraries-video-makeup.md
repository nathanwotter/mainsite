# PRT 358 Libraries Video-Workshop Makeup

Use this special case only when the student's absence matches the PRT 358 class meeting held at the Libraries on planning, recording, and editing video. Confirm the match using the absence date, the current PRT 358 schedule, and the current-semester Moodle course.

This activity-specific assignment replaces the ordinary recording-and-video-response attendance makeup for that class period.

## Source current requirements

- Open the current semester's PRT 358 Moodle course and locate the week containing the missed Libraries workshop.
- Prefer the most recently updated version of Gabriel Graña's workshop slides or instructions when Moodle contains multiple versions.
- Link the email directly to the current semester's Moodle resource for `Workshop with Gabriel Grana from Libraries` and, when used, the current Moodle resource for Gabriel's free-media guide. Do not reuse Moodle activity IDs from a prior semester.
- Check the current slides and assignment before drafting. Adapt the equipment or editing checklist if the current materials have changed; do not treat the Fall 2026 details below as permanently authoritative.

## Makeup requirements

- Explain that the missed session introduced both production skills and the Libraries spaces, resources, and people available to support the student's project.
- State that the makeup is optional and that the student may use the PRT 358 freebie absence instead.
- Ask the student to review the current Gabriel Graña workshop slides in Moodle.
- Have the student visit the DH Hill Digital Media Lab during open hours. This visit is required even if the student already knows how to record and edit the video independently, because becoming familiar with the space and its staff is part of the missed class experience.
- Have the student check out and practice with the recording equipment specified in the current materials. For the Fall 2026 workshop, this included a DJI OM-5 gimbal, Zoom H1n audio recorder, and lavalier microphone.
- For the Fall 2026 makeup, have the student record a few short clips for a brief narrative about themselves. The footage should demonstrate stable recording while moving, remain in focus and consistently oriented, and use the external microphone and recorder instead of the phone's built-in microphone.
- Ask a Digital Media Lab staff member to help the student become familiar with the space and edit the clips in a supported editor such as CapCut or DaVinci Resolve. Do not frame staff assistance as optional merely because the student can edit independently.
- Follow the current workshop activity's editing checklist. In Fall 2026, the demo included:
  1. A music track or sound effect.
  2. A voiceover.
  3. At least one transition.
  4. At least one text box.
  5. A sticker or filter.
- Have the student upload the finished demo to YouTube as **Unlisted**, not Private, and share the link with Nathan.
- Require a selfie with the Digital Media Lab staff member who helped them; this documents the Libraries-space and staff-introduction component of the makeup.
- Explain that the video can be short and does not need to be polished. The purpose is to demonstrate basic equipment and editing skills.
- Use the normal attendance deadline rule. When return timing is unclear, say `within one week after you return`; if Nathan has already chosen a different natural phrasing for the thread, preserve it.
- Invite questions. Historical messages estimate about one hour and 15 minutes for the activity; retain that estimate unless current materials suggest otherwise.

## Voice and layout

Use Nathan's established Adventure Team-style formatting for this structured makeup: keep the greeting and brief acknowledgment together, leave a blank line before the makeup explanation, use a numbered list with one action per item, indent any equipment or editing sublists cleanly, and give the timing note and closing their own paragraphs.
